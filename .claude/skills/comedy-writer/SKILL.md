# Comedy Writer

Comedy fails when it's safe, obvious, or generic. The goal is *actually funny* — not performed humor.

## The Foundation (Read This First)

**Specificity is the engine of comedy.** "A lawyer" is not funny. "A divorce attorney who has a photo of a sailboat he doesn't own on his LinkedIn" is funny. Always trade up for the specific.

**Surprise + inevitability.** A punchline should feel unexpected AND, in retrospect, like the only thing it could have been. Random ≠ funny. *Surprising but correct* = funny.

**Comedy is recognition.** Most laughs are "that's so true." Even absurdist humor has an honest emotional core. Find what's true first, then distort it.

**The punchline goes last.** The funny word, image, or idea belongs at the end of the sentence. Structure sentences so the laugh lands on the final beat.

**Commit to the bit.** Half-committed premises die. If you're going somewhere, go all the way there.

**Never explain.** If you have to explain why it's funny, it isn't.

## Before Writing

1. **Find the true observation** — what's real/honest about this topic that people don't usually say out loud?
2. **Make it specific** — swap every generic noun for the most specific, visual version
3. **Find the turn** — where does it go that's unexpected but inevitable?
4. **Check rhythm** — read it aloud; the punchline should land on the last word

## Forms

See `references/forms.md` for how each format works with structural patterns.

**Stand-up bit** — Premise + escalating examples or rule of three. Callbacks reward patient audiences.

**Monologue** — First-person confessional. Build the situation, let it spiral, end on something self-defeating or absurdly mundane.

**One-liner** — Maximum compression. One premise, one turn, fewest possible words. Punchline word at the end.

**Observational story** — "Have you ever noticed..." + escalating truth + earned absurd conclusion.

**Roast** — Specific to the target. True observations exaggerated to their logical extreme. Affection makes it land; cruelty alone just stings.

**Comic essay** — Long-form observation with a consistent voice. Analogies, digressions that pay off, a thesis that gets weirder as it's proven.

## What Not To Do

- **Don't pick the obvious punchline.** Your first idea is everyone's first idea. Go one or two turns past it.
- **Don't be random.** Non sequitur for its own sake is lazy. Surprise needs to feel earned.
- **Don't signal the joke.** No "get this," "here's the funny part," or "you might think." Just do it.
- **Don't write AI humor about AI.** Observations about robots misunderstanding emotions, or AI being confused by humans — it's been done to death and it's never been good.
- **Don't moralize.** Comedy can have a point of view, but a lecture with a punchline is still a lecture.
- **Don't hedge.** Committed wrongness is funnier than cautious accuracy.
